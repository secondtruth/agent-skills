# Creative Moves

Choose one move that fits the blockage, then return to the user's reaction. These are provocations, not a questionnaire.

## A feeling without words

Offer two moments with distinct emotional mechanisms. “A station where everyone has left” and “a station where everyone is asleep and trusts you” both feel quiet, but invite different play. Ask which detail is warmer. Keep the detail that resonates and explore its consequences.

Use a sensory contrast when adjectives stall: muffled footsteps versus a ringing floor; a body that leans into motion versus one that snaps to attention. Then propose what that contrast changes in the player's behavior.

## A genre without an identity

Change the verb while keeping the world: in the haunted house, listen, repair, host or relocate instead of fight. Explore how each verb changes relationships and the player's power.

Reverse an assumption: the dungeon wants you to survive; the racing route remembers your mistakes; shelter travels while danger stays still. Follow one reversal into an actual choice and its consequence.

## A mood board without a game

Freeze one image, place a player in it, and ask what they can change. Play the next thirty seconds forward. If the image only survives as a cutscene, try a different interaction that makes the same feeling happen through agency.

## Too many promising features

Perform a subtraction: remove combat, crafting, the map or permanent progression, one at a time. Which removal reveals the game's identity, and which destroys it? Preserve the latter interaction; park attractive extras separately.

## Familiar influences taking over

Extract a relationship rather than a feature list: a refuge makes expeditions feel dangerous; bodily commitment makes movement feel consequential. Transplant that relationship into a different activity and test the resulting scene.

## A clever mechanic that feels empty

Try the same mechanic under two emotional promises. Passing an object might express trust, contamination, inheritance or theft. Change who needs the object and what handing it over costs. Let the user choose the relationship worth developing.
