# Concept Brief

Use the parts that answer the user's request. A short concept can fit on one page; a request for three pitches deserves three short pitches. Preserve the user's resonant wording.

- **Working title and promise:** who the player becomes, what they do, and the distinctive experience this creates.
- **Vibe anchors:** a few concrete sensory or emotional anchors, each connected to a design choice. Include confirmed mismatches if they help protect the direction.
- **Playable moment:** a short present-tense scene with player agency, feedback and consequences.
- **Core loop and variation:** what repeats, what changes, and why the next repetition could remain interesting.
- **Design pillars:** a few rules that resolve actual design choices, such as “safety is something you carry.” Show one consequence of each.
- **PC/console fit and scope:** relevant input, camera, readability and session assumptions; preserve the central experience in the smallest plausible version.
- **Unknowns and next experiment:** the most consequential uncertainty and the cheapest way to learn about it.

Separate confirmed decisions, proposed choices and unresolved questions. For an open decision, state the question, plausible options and a recommendation; leave authorship with the user.

## An experiment that can teach something

State the hypothesis, setup and observable evidence. For example:

“Does carrying shelter create attachment or just slow travel? Build one short exposed path, one optional detour and a movable shelter with simple sound and motion feedback. Observe whether players deliberately position the shelter and explain why they care about it, or abandon it whenever possible. Their choices and explanations decide whether to deepen the relationship or revise the movement cost.”

Pick a graybox, paper model, role-play or audiovisual sketch according to the uncertainty. A mood sketch can test aesthetic resonance; movement feel needs an interactive test. Report the experiment as proposed until it has actually run.
