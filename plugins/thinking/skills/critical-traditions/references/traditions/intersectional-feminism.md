# Intersectional Feminism

## What this tradition sees

How systems of oppression (gender, race, class, sexuality, ability, citizenship) interlock rather than stack additively. The lens insists that the experience of, say, a Black working-class woman is not "racism + sexism + class disadvantage" but a distinct position with its own analytical needs that single-axis analysis misses.

## Core concepts

- **Intersectionality** – categories of identity and oppression as mutually constituting, not independent variables
- **Matrix of domination** (Hill Collins) – interlocking systems rather than hierarchies of oppression
- **Standpoint epistemology** – knowledge produced from marginalized positions reveals what dominant positions cannot see
- **Single-axis analysis** – the failure mode where one axis (gender alone, race alone) erases people at intersections
- **Erasure / hypervisibility** – paired modes by which intersectional subjects are mishandled by single-axis movements

## Key questions

- Who is being analytically erased when this topic is framed in single-axis terms?
- What patterns emerge specifically at intersections that no single category captures?
- Whose experience is treated as the default ("women" often = white middle-class women; "workers" often = male)?
- What does the topic look like from standpoints that are not centered?

## Characteristic moves

1. Disaggregate the category being used. "Women" → which women, where, what class, what race, what citizenship?
2. Locate people at intersections and ask whether the analysis serves them.
3. Note where solidarity is being constructed across axes versus where solidarity is being demanded at the cost of erasing axis.

## Internal contestation

Debate over whether intersectionality is a *method* (a way of doing analysis) or a *theory* (a substantive claim about identity); whether it is best deployed legally (Crenshaw's original frame) or sociologically; whether it can be extended indefinitely without losing analytical traction. Some materialist feminists argue that intersectional language has been depoliticized in mainstream use, reduced to identity-additionism.

## Known weaknesses

Can become a checklist of identities applied procedurally rather than an analytical practice. The legal-origin specificity sometimes does not travel well to other domains. The tradition can also under-engage with structural-economic analysis if "class" is treated as just another identity axis.

## Primary sources

- Kimberlé Crenshaw, "Demarginalizing the Intersection of Race and Sex" (1989) and "Mapping the Margins" (1991)
- bell hooks, "Ain't I a Woman?" (1981); "Feminism is for Everybody" (2000)
- Patricia Hill Collins, "Black Feminist Thought" (1990)
- Audre Lorde, "Sister Outsider" (1984)

## Especially useful for

Questions about policy, social movements, organizational design, representation – any situation where general-category claims about a group hide differential effects within the group.

## Less useful for

Highly abstract or apolitical technical questions where identity-axis effects are not in play. Forcing application produces hollow output.
