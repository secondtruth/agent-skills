# Materialist Feminism

## What this tradition sees

Gender oppression as a structural product of material conditions – the organization of labor, property, reproduction, and the household – rather than primarily as a matter of culture, identity, or attitudes. Patriarchy is treated as a system with an economic logic, intertwined with but not reducible to capitalism.

## Core concepts

- **Reproductive labor** – the unpaid work (childbearing, child-rearing, care, domestic maintenance) that sustains the workforce but is structurally invisible in capitalist accounting
- **Primitive accumulation of women** (Federici) – historical violence (witch-hunts, enclosure of bodies) that established the gendered division of labor
- **Social reproduction** – the broader system that produces and reproduces labor power over generations
- **Wages for housework** – the strategic demand that revealed the structural dependency of waged labor on unwaged reproductive labor
- **Dual systems theory** – the analytical claim that patriarchy and capitalism are distinct systems that intersect

## Key questions

- What unpaid or under-paid labor sustains the situation being analyzed?
- Who does the reproductive work that makes this possible?
- How is the "private sphere" being treated as natural / outside analysis when it is actually structured?
- What material interests are organized by the current gendered arrangement, and who benefits from its persistence?

## Characteristic moves

1. Identify the labor that is being treated as not-labor (love, care, family, "natural" female activity).
2. Trace how that labor connects to the visible economy – what would collapse if it stopped?
3. Examine the historical formation of the current arrangement – it is rarely as old or as natural as it claims.
4. Resist the move that explains structural outcomes by individual choices ("women choose to do more care work").

## Internal contestation

Disagreement over whether patriarchy is a *separate* system from capitalism (dual systems) or whether they are *one* integrated system (unitary theory). Disagreement over how the analysis extends to non-cis-women. Tension with intersectional feminism over the centrality of class versus the irreducibility of other axes.

## Known weaknesses

Can underplay the cultural-symbolic dimension of gender that does not reduce cleanly to material structure. Historically Eurocentric in many formulations (Mies and Federici are partial correctives). Sometimes economically determinist – treating consciousness as epiphenomenal of material conditions.

## Primary sources

- Silvia Federici, "Caliban and the Witch" (2004); "Wages Against Housework" (1975)
- Maria Mies, "Patriarchy and Accumulation on a World Scale" (1986)
- Lise Vogel, "Marxism and the Oppression of Women" (1983)
- Tithi Bhattacharya (ed.), "Social Reproduction Theory" (2017)

## Especially useful for

Care work, household economics, labor markets, reproductive politics, welfare state design, anything where unpaid labor is doing analytical heavy lifting under another name.

## Less useful for

Topics where the gendered material structure is not the dominant axis – e.g. cultural representation questions where intersectional or post-structural lenses cut deeper.
