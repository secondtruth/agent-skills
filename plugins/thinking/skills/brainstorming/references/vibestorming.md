# Vibestorming: Iterative Creative Articulation

## What It Is

Vibestorming is an iterative process where a human and AI collaborate to capture, develop, and articulate thoughts that the human can *feel* but can't yet express precisely. It differs from regular brainstorming in that the goal isn't generating new ideas from scratch, but rather excavating and crystallizing ideas that already exist in the person's mind.

The name combines "Vibe" (the intuitive, emotional essence of what someone wants to say) with "Storming" (the dynamic, iterative process of getting there).

## The Core Loop

### 1. Initiation
The user provides a starting point – often vague, incomplete, or emotional rather than logical. Examples:
- "I want to say something about X, but I don't know how"
- "There's this feeling I have about my situation that I can't put into words"
- "I need to describe myself for a profile, but everything I write sounds generic"

### 2. Resonance Building
Draw on everything available – the current conversation, known context about the user, shared documents, previous interactions – to make an informed first attempt. The attempt doesn't need to be perfect. It needs to be specific enough to provoke a reaction.

### 3. The Warm-Cold Game
This is the heart of Vibestorming. After each attempt:
- The user signals what resonates ("warm") and what doesn't ("cold")
- Feedback can be explicit ("too formal") or implicit ("hmm, not quite")
- Even rejection is valuable data – it narrows the space

Pay attention to *what specifically* the user reacts to. If they like one phrase in a paragraph but reject the rest, that phrase is an anchor point to build from.

### 4. Iterative Refinement
Each round gets closer. The process typically moves through phases:
- **Rough direction** (first 1-2 rounds): Getting the general tone and intent right
- **Fine-tuning** (rounds 3-4): Nailing specific word choices, phrasing, emotional register
- **Polish** (final round): Small adjustments, the user's voice fully coming through

### 5. Crystallization
The end state: something that makes the user say "yes, that's exactly what I mean" or "that sounds like *me*." The result should feel like the user wrote it themselves, not like AI-generated text with their name on it.

## Key Principles

**Authenticity over polish.** A slightly rough formulation that sounds like the user beats a perfectly crafted sentence that sounds like a chatbot. Always prioritize voice over virtuosity.

**Specificity provokes reaction.** Vague proposals get vague feedback. Make bold, specific choices in early drafts – even if they're wrong, they give the user something concrete to react to.

**The user is the authority.** They know what they mean, even if they can't say it yet. Trust their gut reactions. If they say "not quite" but can't explain why, try a different angle rather than asking them to articulate the problem.

**Context is everything.** The more you know about the user's situation, personality, and communication style, the faster you converge. Use all available context aggressively.

**Don't converge too fast.** Sometimes the early rounds should explore different directions rather than refining the first attempt. Offer contrasting options to help the user discover what they want.

## Practical Applications

Vibestorming works especially well for:

- **Self-descriptions and profiles**: Capturing personality authentically in writing
- **Emotional articulation**: Putting complex feelings into words
- **Communication drafting**: Messages where tone matters as much as content
- **Creative writing**: Finding the right voice for a character or piece
- **Personal branding**: Expressing identity without sounding corporate or generic

## Example Flow

```
User: "I need a bio for the conference page. Something that says what I do
       without sounding like a LinkedIn headline."

AI: "Two directions — tell me which one is warmer:
     A) 'Builds small tools for people who run their own servers.'
     B) 'Spends most days convincing computers to be less annoying.'"

User: "A is closer. But 'small tools' undersells it."

AI: "So the scale matters, the tone doesn't. Next round:
     A) 'Builds the tooling that lets small teams run real infrastructure.'
     B) 'Makes self-hosting boring — in the good way.'"

User: "B! That's it. Keep 'in the good way'."
```

Each round narrows on what the user reacts to — here: scale, then a phrase — until the words match the feeling.
