# Analytical Lenses

## When to use which mode

- **Single lens**: User names one explicitly ("do a stakeholder map", "what are the externalities here"). Apply that lens only.
- **Selected set**: Complex topic, no specific lens named. Choose 3–5 lenses most relevant to the situation. Always include at least one that the user would not have asked for themselves (the point is to surface blind spots).
- **Full sweep**: User asks for thorough analysis or explicitly wants all relevant angles. Apply all eight, but compress lenses with little to say to one or two sentences rather than padding.

## The lenses

Each lens lives in its own reference file. Read the lens file first — the questions and moves are the lens.

- `references/lenses/stakeholder-mapping.md` – Who is in the room, who is not, who is affected without participating
- `references/lenses/power-analysis.md` – Who decides, who benefits, who pays, who is dependent
- `references/lenses/externalities.md` – Costs and benefits pushed outside the frame
- `references/lenses/temporal-shifts.md` – Short vs. long term, historical context, irreversibility
- `references/lenses/scale-shifts.md` – Individual vs. institutional vs. systemic level
- `references/lenses/value-conflicts.md` – Competing values made explicit rather than treated as one
- `references/lenses/counterfactual-reasoning.md` – What would absence or alternatives look like
- `references/lenses/steelman-objection.md` – The strongest case against, articulated charitably

## Output structure

For multi-lens analyses, structure the output by lens with brief headings. After applying lenses, write a **Synthesis** section that integrates what the lenses revealed – tensions between them, convergent findings, and the most action-relevant takeaways. Without synthesis, multi-lens analysis is just a list.

## Quality standards

- **One line when a lens has little to say.** Say so and move on; forced application produces noise.
- **Self-critical.** Each lens has known blind spots. Surface them when the lens is applied – not as ritual disclaimer, but as honest scoping ("this lens systematically underweights X").
- **Lenses surface considerations; the synthesis carries the recommendation,** clearly marked as such.

Done when every applied lens has produced at least one observation specific to this topic and the Synthesis names the tensions between lenses.

## Composition with `critical-traditions`

For topics with substantive normative dimensions, the methodical lenses here surface the *structure* of the situation; the `critical-traditions` skill, when it is among your available skills, provides *normative interpretations* of it. Map the structure first — stakeholder-mapping + power-analysis — then apply one or more traditions for value-laden interpretation; without that skill, name the normative frame you apply in the synthesis.
