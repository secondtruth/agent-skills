# Stakeholder Mapping

## What this lens sees

Who is in the room, who is not, and who is affected by the outcome without participating in the decision. The lens surfaces the gap between *participants*, *affected parties*, and *decision-makers* – three groups that rarely overlap perfectly.

## Key questions

- Who is directly involved in this decision or situation?
- Who is affected by the outcome but not in the conversation?
- Whose voice is structurally absent (children, future generations, non-humans, the dead, the not-yet-organized)?
- Who is implicitly represented by proxy (e.g., "users" represented by product managers)? Is the proxy reliable?
- Who has the most to lose? The most to gain? Are those the same people?
- Who would object if they knew? Who would notice if they knew?

## Characteristic moves

1. List the obvious stakeholders.
2. List the non-obvious ones – the second-order affected, the silenced, the unborn, the displaced.
3. Mark each stakeholder by their level of agency in the situation (decides / participates / is consulted / is affected silently).
4. Identify gaps where representation is structurally impossible and consider whether the situation requires a proxy or rule to compensate.

## Known blind spots

This lens is **structural**, not normative. It will tell you who is silent but not whether their silence is okay. It can also overproduce – listing too many indirectly affected parties dilutes focus. Cap the analysis at the stakeholders whose interests would *meaningfully shift the decision* if surfaced.

## Output format

A table or list with: stakeholder | role (decider / participant / consulted / affected) | what they have at stake | how their interest is currently represented (if at all).
