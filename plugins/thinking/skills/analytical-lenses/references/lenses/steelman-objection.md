# Steelman & Strongest Objection

## What this lens sees

The strongest available argument against the position currently being held, articulated charitably and at full force. The lens is a discipline against arguing with the weakest version of opposition (the strawman) – which is the default failure mode of confident thinking.

## Key questions

- What is the best argument against this position? Not the one most people make – the *best* one.
- Who is the most thoughtful, informed critic of this view? What do they say?
- If a smart person disagreed with this position, what would they have noticed that I have not?
- Where is the current position weakest? What is it least able to defend?
- What is the strongest version of the opposing position, granted its best assumptions and most charitable interpretation?

## Characteristic moves

1. **Find the actual opposition**, not a caricature. Real critics, real arguments, in their own words if possible.
2. **Grant the opposition its best premises.** Even premises you disagree with. The point is to test whether the position survives a strong attack, not to confirm it against a weak one.
3. **Articulate the opposition's case in its own terms**, not in terms designed to make it sound bad.
4. **Then respond**, after the steelman is fully built – or notice that the steelman is harder to answer than expected and update.

## Known blind spots

Steelmanning a clearly wrong or harmful position can produce false symmetry – making it seem like the question is "balanced" when one side actually has the evidence. Use judgment: the lens is for positions where reasonable people disagree, not for laundering bad-faith claims into respectable ones. Also, doing this well requires genuinely knowing the opposing view; if the steelman is shallow, the test is weak.

## Output format

Two short sections: **The strongest case against** (in the opposition's own terms, no rebuttal yet) and **Response** (engaging with the steelman directly, conceding where appropriate). If the steelman is strong enough to require updating the original position, say so plainly.
