# Externalities

## What this lens sees

Costs and benefits that are produced by a decision but borne by parties outside the decision frame. The lens treats every situation as accounting and asks: *what is not on the balance sheet but should be?*

## Key questions

- Whose costs are not counted because they are not paid in money?
- What environmental, social, or psychological costs are absorbed silently?
- Are there benefits that accrue to parties who did not contribute to producing them? (Positive externalities count too.)
- What costs are pushed forward in time – paid later by someone who has no say now?
- What costs are pushed sideways – exported to other regions, populations, ecosystems?
- If everyone affected had to be compensated, would this decision still make sense?

## Characteristic moves

1. List the direct costs and benefits (the accounting visible to participants).
2. Identify what is missing from that accounting: pollution, mental load, relational damage, learning lost, time displaced, options foreclosed.
3. Identify who bears each externality.
4. Distinguish unavoidable externalities (must be borne by someone) from designed-in ones (could be internalized with a different structure).

## Known blind spots

The lens is most useful when there is a clear "frame" being looked outside of. For diffuse situations with no clear decision frame, externalities analysis can become an exercise in listing everything bad that ever happens. It also tends to assume costs are bad and benefits are good without examining the values implicit in those judgments – pair with `value-conflicts` when this matters.

## Output format

Two columns: **Internalized** (already in the decision frame) and **Externalized** (paid by outside parties). For each externalized item, note who pays.
